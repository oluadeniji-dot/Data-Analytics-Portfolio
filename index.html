02 / Key takeaways

What the full dataset shows

31.2% reported zero visits

6,308 of 20,190 records contain no outpatient physician visits. The distribution is heavily skewed, so averages alone hide important variation.

Differences across health groups

Records rated “Poor” average 5.79 visits, compared with 2.63 for “Excellent.” Segment sizes vary substantially.

A small high-use group matters

9.2% of records show 8 or more visits, yet these rows account for 42.6% of all visits. Useful as an illustration of workload concentration.

03 / Reproducible workflow

From source data to decision-ready reporting

A simple, auditable workflow suitable for a data analyst portfolio.

STEP 01

Source

Load the public RAND HIE subset distributed by the statsmodels package.

STEP 02

Validate

Check nulls, row keys, nonnegative counts, and mutually exclusive health flags.

STEP 03

Transform

Derive a readable health category and reconstruct coinsurance from a logged field.

STEP 04

Communicate

Cross-check SQL/Python summaries, then publish Excel, a report, and a filtered dashboard.

Scope and analytic judgment

Metric definitions matter. One row in this subset is not necessarily one unique individual; all averages and shares use records as the denominator.

Dates matter. This source is from a historical U.S. experiment (1974–1982), so it is not a current demand forecast or a clinical recommendation.

Tool claims matter. This deliverable includes Python, SQLite SQL, and an Excel summary. Power BI/Tableau skills are separate professional experience, not implied by a file we did not create.
